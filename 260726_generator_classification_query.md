# Query for auditors: how should Engine Type be recorded for generators?

**26 July 2026 | NRMM LEZ audit data analysis**

We are analysing the NRMM audit records and would welcome your guidance on the `Engine Type` field for generators. Among the 218 generators recorded at Stage V, none carries Engine Type "Constant". Among Stage IIIA generators audited in the same years, 80% do (303 of 379).

We recognise that not all generators run at constant speed. Hybrid, flywheel and flybrid units in the data are split 37 "Constant" to 51 "Variable", which looks like a deliberate distinction. Separately, 17 of roughly 85 repeat-audited generators carry both labels at different visits. Median engine size is almost identical either way (132 kW against 137 kW).

Our questions, in the hope that the pattern has a straightforward explanation we have missed:

- Is there written guidance on setting `Engine Type` for a generator, and did that guidance or its interpretation change around 2022, when Stage V units began appearing on site?
- Does the Stage V type-approval documentation itself state constant or variable speed, and is that wording what auditors transcribe into the field?
- Should a genset running at fixed rpm to produce mains-frequency power be recorded as "Constant" regardless of how its certification is worded?
- For hybrid, inverter or flywheel units, which label do you intend auditors to use?
- When the same machine is audited more than once, is `Engine Type` re-entered from the plate each time, or carried forward from the earlier record?
- Is `Engine Type` used on site to decide which emissions standard applies, or is it recorded for reference only?

The field matters to us because it determines which Machine Group a machine falls into, and therefore which threshold applies. Read literally, it implies generators have not improved since 2019; read differently, it implies they are improving quickly. Any note on intended practice, or a rule you would like us to apply, would let us classify consistently rather than guess.
