## Known limitations

1. **Ecological inference:** aggregate audit counts approximate individual machine transition hazards.
2. **Entry/exit bias:** audit sample records new and continuing machines; Markov time-homogeneity is violated as the underlying fleet composition changes.
3. **Single annual probability:** lambda_CF is assumed constant over Phase C; time-varying hazard within Phase B is documented but not carried forward.
4. **Counterfactual overestimate:** lambda_CF likely overstates natural turnover due to second-hand market leakage; lambda_Proactive is a conservative lower bound.
5. **Phase A1/A2 sparsity:** Variable_Speed lambda is estimated from pooled A1/A2 data.
6. **Scenario B is an upper bound:** full enforcement effectiveness assumed; actual outcomes depend on audit frequency, capacity, and operator response time.
7. **Emissions index only:** no fleet size input. EF_s from EMEP/EEA Tier 3 at nominal load; real-world emissions depend on duty cycle.

## Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Scenario A** | Baseline forecast: P_Total applied annually (natural turnover + proactive LEZ). No enforcement augmentation. |
| **Scenario B** | Upper-bound forecast: Boolean mask zeroes transitions to non-compliant stages; rescales rows. Non-compliant machines must jump to Stage V each step. |
| **Boolean mask** | Set P_Total[i,j] = 0 for all j < compliance threshold for year t; rescale rows. |
| **pi_0** | Phase C initialisation distribution: Stage proportions from P24 cold-engaged records. |
| **Compliance threshold** | Minimum Stage integer per group per year (schema.md Table 2). CS = 6 throughout; VS = 5 (2025–2029), 6 (2030). |
| **Emissions index** | E_t / E_2025 × 100; E_t = Σ_s(pi_s,t × kW_s × 2000 hr × EF_s). |
| **EF_s** | EMEP/EEA Tier 3 NOx emission factor (g NOx/kWh) for Stage s (Ntziachristos & Samaras 2019). |
| **kW_s** | Mean rated power of machines at Stage s in Phase C cold fleet, from audit records. |

#### 5.2 — Scenario A: Stage distribution forecast

Under Scenario A (no enforcement), Stage V rises monotonically for both groups at a rate governed by p_cf and p_pro. Constant_Speed growth is slow (p_cf = 0.016, p_pro = 0.000): Stage V rises from 53.8% in 2025 to 57.4% in 2030 (+3.6 pp over 5 years). The Stage IIIA tail persists throughout (38.5% → 35.5%), consistent with the near-zero CS replacement rate. Variable_Speed growth is faster (p_cf = 0.168, p_pro = 0.002): Stage V rises from 76.5% to 90.8% (+14.3 pp), with Stage IV declining from 13.6% to 5.4% and Stage IIIB from 7.7% to 3.0%.

![Scenario A stage distribution forecast](260417_step5_forecasting_v1_fig5_2_scen_a_stage.png)

Stage V rises monotonically under Scenario A for both groups; the rate for CS (~0.7 pp/yr) is an order of magnitude slower than VS (~2.9 pp/yr), directly reflecting the lower p_cf.
Stage IIIA in CS is a persistent residual — at current replacement rates it will not be absorbed by 2030 without enforcement intervention.

#### 5.3 — Scenario B: Enforcement upper bound

Under full enforcement (Scenario B), the Boolean mask zeroes all transitions to stages below the year-specific compliance threshold and rescales rows. For Constant_Speed (threshold = Stage V = 6 throughout), all non-compliant machines must transition to Stage V in the first annual step: Stage V jumps from 53.8% in 2025 to 100.0% in 2026 and remains there. For Variable_Speed, the 2025–2029 threshold is Stage IV (integer 5): Stages I–IIIB (totalling ~9.9% in 2025) are forced to Stage V in 2026, but Stage IV (13.6% in 2025) is permitted to remain, declining naturally through 2029. In 2030 the threshold upgrades to Stage V, forcing the remaining Stage IV fraction to Stage V: 100.0% compliance from 2030.

![Scenario B stage distribution forecast](260417_step5_forecasting_v1_fig5_3_scen_b_stage.png)

Under full enforcement, Constant_Speed achieves 100% Stage V compliance by 2026; Variable_Speed eliminates Stages I–IIIB immediately but retains Stage IV until the 2030 threshold uplift.
The stepwise threshold change for VS (IV→V in 2030) is visible as a second compliance jump, correctly capturing the phased policy tightening.

#### 5.4 — Compliance trajectory

Scenario A: Constant_Speed compliance (at Stage V threshold) rises from 53.8% to 57.4% by 2030 — a 3.6 pp gain, indicating that natural turnover alone is insufficient to achieve full compliance on the CS generator fleet within the forecast horizon. Variable_Speed compliance (at Stage IV threshold 2025–2029, Stage V in 2030) rises from 90.1% to 95.3% by 2029, then drops to 90.8% in 2030 when the threshold upgrades to Stage V — the Stage IV fraction (~5.4% in 2029) falls out of compliance, which is correctly captured. Scenario B: both groups achieve 100% compliance by 2026 (CS) and 2030 (VS) respectively under full enforcement.

![Compliance trajectory 2025–2030](260417_step5_forecasting_v1_fig5_4_compliance.png)

Scenario A (blue) vs Scenario B (red dashed): the wide gap for CS illustrates that enforcement is essential for the generator fleet — natural turnover alone delivers only ~4 pp improvement by 2030.
The VS Scenario A compliance drop in 2030 is analytically correct and reflects the policy tightening from Stage IV to Stage V at the era-4 boundary.

The 2025 compliance rate for CS (53.8%) is the observed P24 audit snapshot; only 46.2% of audited generators already met the Stage V requirement at Phase C entry.
The enforcement-attributable improvement ceiling by 2030 is 42.6 pp for CS and 9.2 pp for VS — confirming that CS is the group most dependent on enforcement for compliance.
